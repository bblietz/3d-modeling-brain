Brian's garage has two wooden combs (1x4 on edge, screwed to the side face of exposed 2x8-looking joists, 27 in long, 20 in hang, half-round 1-1/4 in notches at about 5 in) holding rods horizontally above the open garage door. Rods stay at rest but any bump while pulling a neighbour drops one: a half-round in a vertical edge has its low point at the board edge.

Decided 2026-11-07: "use the deep hook design", "make individual deep hooks, that can be screwed into a 1x4 piece of wood" (a blend of the printed comb and the caps-on-wood option); then "make the bottom resting part of the hook have a slot that is angled", "remove the top most part of the device that is not over the wood", correcting my first attempt, "the slot should angle away from the wood", "5 holders should fit in the 20 in, no space between each hook as it sits on the wood", and "print these vertically so there can be much more in a single print". Built: `projects/Fishing-rod-holder/rod_hook.py`, an open J 4 x 4 x 3/8 in (height = 20 in hang / 5): tab on the board with two #8 x 1 screws 1 in from the edge, jaw 2-1/4 past the edge with a 7/8 slot leaning 35 degrees away from the wood; lift to leave 1.57 in for 1/4 to 5/8 blanks (pose-space check in the script).

2026-11-09, the second version: "There should be 2 versions of the holder. One that we already have is for the rod tip end. The rod butt end should be able to accommodate a 2 in rod handle. The rods I have are 1 7/8 in or so. Make it a 2 in opening." "Make both versions in the same file, vertical." "The holders will stack on top of each other, make sure there is 2 in of room to get the butt in." Built as `rh.BUTT` in the same file (`rh.TIP` is the tip hook; a `Hook` object each): 4-1/2 x 4 x 3/8, 2 in slot leaning 10 degrees, floor 5/16, lip tip at 1-3/4 so the grip has 2-1/4 in to pass under the hook stacked above (asserted at 2 or more), lift to leave 1.405 in for 1-1/2 to 1-7/8 grips, no hood (the hold is all lift). The 4 in pitch is the budget: lip tip + grip + clearance <= 4, and 10 degrees is the most lean that leaves the lip a straight inner face. The hold check now includes the hooks above and below and the board as obstacles for both hooks.

Later on 2026-11-09: "there should only be 5 pairs" (five of each), "all edges should have a very slight roundover", then "do not add a round over to the back of the part. Use this flat surface to print the part vertically." Built: `EDGE_R` 1/32 on the front face's outline and countersink rims (one `fillet()` over an outline now made of lines and true arcs; the polygon version could not take a 3D fillet), back face and bottom strip square, asserted by face area; the overhang check reads the standing STL layer by layer at 0.3 mm (`section_multiplane`), since facet normals call a small bed round 90 degrees when it prints as a 40 degree step. The second sentence was read as "stand on the square bottom edge as before"; if Brian meant lying on the flat back it is a 2 x 2 grid, 4 per plate, 3 plates (noted in the brief and handoff, not built).

`rod-hook.3mf`: ONE project file, ONE plate, five pairs in one row at 23 mm pitch (tip hooks at the back, butt hooks in front): 359.2 g / 7 h 56 (35.9 g, 48 min each with pads), pads proven 0.873 to 0.89 vs 0.14, round trip keeps the plate; `PLATES` in `pipeline/make_print_3mf.py` maps each hook to a plate ({"tip": 1, "butt": 2} gives the earlier two-plate layout). Options page https://claude.ai/artifact/MuJqbgWZjUNzqSTMLFRt4H (Version 7: both hooks, roundover and flat faces in the checks, stacked-gap close-up, two-board drilling guide, one-plate table). Not printed; five butted hooks per board (bottoms 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 below the joist) assume reels off or pointing away from the board; comb spacing still unknown; the fattest grip should be measured before printing the five butt hooks (2 in opening, 1/16 per side on a 1-7/8 grip; `slot_w` is one constant).

**How to apply:** when Brian says a slot or hook is "angled", state which way the low end goes before building (this cost one cycle). A second size of the same hook inside a fixed pitch is a height budget first: write the one inequality (tip + part + clearance <= pitch) before touching the lean. When he names a surface of a standing part ("the back"), say which face you took it to be and what the other reading would change before the pipeline runs; a roundover request on a printed part always comes with "which faces stay flat" (bed face, mating face). After the print, record the real fits and the feel of the up-and-out motion for both hooks in `knowledge/learnings/fishing-rod-holder.md`; a mirrored hook is one line away if a board needs both edges.
