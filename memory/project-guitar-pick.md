Plain PETG copy of the Dunlop Tortex Standard 1.0 mm pick (351 shape), `projects/Guitar-pick/`. On 2026-10-11 Brian measured it at 26.19 x 30.38 x 1.0 mm; the outline was traced from his two caliper photos (`pipeline/trace_photo.py`) and he approved it on the sign-off page. The measured tip radius is 4.5 mm; the generic 351 SVG outline is 3.4 mm, so the generic shape is NOT a stand-in for the Dunlop.

On 2026-10-12 Brian rejected the square edge ("the pick edges should be tapered"), saw round, chamfer and bevel drawn at scale, chose "like the bevel, but no noticeable line, so a curved lead in" (built as `soft`: 1.5 mm bevel, crease blended R 5), then asked for it mirrored on both faces with no support material. A flat print's underside carries about 38 degrees at most (0.06 layers, 0.22 line: layer 2 at half a line), so flat and the mirrored 1.5 mm bevel are incompatible. Brian chose the stand-up print on a snap-off base ("smoothest surface for PETG") and the rim rounded too (R 0.15). `standup.py` builds the pick tip-up on a neck along the top edge and shoulders to the 45 degree point, a saddle block, a plate and a 4 mm cooling column, one solid, 34.66 mm = 577 layers, with a 2 micron seam strip down the rear face that `pipeline/make_standup_3mf.py` paints as the seam region (`paint_seam="4"`). Research: a new Tortex Standard is die-stamped square and wears into a bevel; no published edge numbers.

First print 2026-10-12 (0.22 mm one-line neck, 0.30 rim, bevels dropping 0.35): 0.2 nozzle, 0.06 layers, 250 C / 70 C, PETG Basic black, finished in 2 h 11 min as estimated (12.4 s per pick layer, the minimum layer time), no errors, the neck held. Brian 2026-10-13: the top edge's two rounded corners "had small chunks missing", "the rim didn't tear, it was a bad print". Cause, from the G-code: on the sloped shoulders the neck sits beside the pick's edge in each layer, and a one-line rim is where the two face walls merge into one bead, so the neck was a spur off that bead; the slicer printed it as a separate 0.3 to 1.3 mm path ending on the pick's edge and seamed the loop there, on all 55 shoulder layers. Fix (built and slice-verified 2026-10-13, NOT printed): rim 0.44 (bevel drop 0.28 per face) and neck 0.40, both two lines, so one closed wall loop runs from the face along the neck into the block; the block's rear face painted as a seam enforcer too; the pipeline now asserts zero path ends at the pick's edge in the neck zone (`pipeline/toolpath.py`). Brian has not yet seen the rim change; it is on the page.

**Why:** Brian wants it as smooth as possible, hence the 0.2 nozzle and the standing print (faces and bevels as walls). Printed flat, any underside taper must stay about 38 degrees or steeper. A break-away tab that meets a part's edge in-plane must be two lines wide, and so must the edge, or the slicer ends a path on the part every layer.

**How to apply:** blend any crease a hand will touch; decide edge profile and print orientation together for thin symmetric parts; pin seams with painted strips; verify toolpaths by path starts and ends, not only loop starts. Next: Brian prints `guitar-pick.3mf` (second file), snaps the block off by bending it away, sands the top edge and shoulders, and reports the corners, the neck break and the feel vs the Tortex; if the break runs into the rim, NECK_T 0.37 is the floor.
