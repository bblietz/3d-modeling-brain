# Fishing rod holder: retrospective (round one 2026-11-07, butt hook 2026-11-09, 1.5 in hook 2026-11-11)

Garage joist rod storage. Brian's two notched 1x4 combs dropped a rod whenever a neighbour was bumped. The replacement is a printed deep-hook pocket, one per rod, screwed to the face of a 1x4 that hangs from the joist as before. See [[../../projects/Fishing-rod-holder/brief|the brief]].

## What worked

- **Diagnose the geometry before drawing fixes.** A half-round notch cut into a vertical edge has its lowest point at the edge, so the rod rests at the mouth with nothing in front of it. Steady state holds by a sliver of slope; any bump frees it. Once that was said in one drawing, every option followed from "put the floor inside the plate behind a lip".
- **One parameter set, three outputs.** `pipeline/concepts.py` held the pocket numbers and drew the to-scale elevations (matplotlib), the concept solids (build123d) and the OpenSCAD scene renders from the same constants; `rod_hook.py` then reused the same numbers for the real part. Nothing on the page disagreed with the CAD.
- **Pose-space hold check as an assert.** `escape_lift(d)` erodes the free space by the rod radius (shapely) and binary-searches the lowest ceiling on the rod centre that still connects the seat to the outside. It returned 1.00 in for 1/4, 3/8 and 5/8 blanks, matching the designed mouth height, and it would have caught a lip that was secretly a ramp. Cost: a few lines. Gotcha: the seat point must sit a hair (0.002 in) above the arc's low point, because the arc is a polygon and the eroded region's lowest vertex lands about 1e-5 above the exact value.
- **OpenSCAD renders of the real STL** (import + context cubes + `rotate([90,0,0])` so model y is up) gave Brian clean pictures of the part on its board and on the joist. The matplotlib iso renders from `render_stl.py` stay as the agent-side check only: a 6 in rod stub made the comb a sliver inside a huge gridded axis box.
- **Real slice numbers on the options page** (`pipeline/slice_stls.py`: flatten presets, CLI export, graft, read `slice_info.config`) settled the material and build decisions without estimates: comb 154 g vs caps 20 g each vs hook 24.6 g.
- **Brian's pick was a blend, not a card.** He took the deep hook (option B) and combined build options A and B into individual hooks on a plain 1x4. Offering the cheap retrofit honestly beside the full print made the hybrid visible.

## What did not

- The first comb concept needed three spliced pieces because 256 mm is 10 in; the hybrid on a wood backbone made the splice problem disappear. Long hanging parts should start from "what carries the length" (wood, rail, extrusion) before a splice design.
- A late `Edit` that appended a function in the middle of `main()` silently swallowed the following block into the new function (indentation). Re-read the tail of a file after inserting a `def` into it.

- **"Angled" has a direction.** Brian asked for an angled resting slot; I sloped the floor down toward the board (rod in the back corner). He meant the slot should lean away from the wood (rod tucked outward under the lip, coming back up the ramp to leave). Before building a tilt, state which way the low end goes in one sentence or draw both and ask. The rework cost one full cycle (CAD, renders, slice, page).
- A build123d `fillet()` loop on hand-picked vertical edges worked but made the 2D check approximate its radii; constructing every fillet as an arc in the outline polygon (`rounded_outline`) and extruding that gave one source of truth for the CAD, the STL and the shapely checks. With a leaning slot the geometry is a handful of trig lines (`S_IN`, `X_C`, `S_OUT`, `X_LIP_TOP`), all derived from the lean, the slot width and the lip height.
- **A polygon outline cannot take a 3D roundover.** When Brian asked for a slight roundover on the edges (2026-11-09), the extruded polygon had about 150 planar side faces with 0.2 mm facets on the R 1/8 arcs: no kernel fillets that. The fix kept the one-source-of-truth idea but changed the representation: the outline is now a path of corners and arcs (`rounded_path`), which `path_face` builds from lines and true arcs for the CAD and `path_points` samples for shapely. The extruded solid then has about 60 faces and `fillet()` rolls 1/32 round the front face's loop in one call. The pose-space numbers did not change, which is the regression test. Two tessellation details: a fillet that ends in a spherical corner leaves a zero-area triangle at the pole, which trimesh counts as an open edge (re-save the STL without degenerate faces), and filleting a smooth (tangent) vertical edge makes OCC refuse, so select only sharp edges.
- **Facet normals overstate a small round at the bed.** A 1/32 quarter-round along a bed edge has facets facing straight down (90 degrees), yet it slices as a 0.25 mm step between the first two 0.3 mm layers (40 degrees). The overhang check now sections the standing STL at every layer height (`section_multiplane`) and measures how far each layer's outline steps past the one below, holes and the first layer excluded; that is what the slicer sees. It also told how much round the bed takes: 1/32 at 40 degrees, 1 mm at 46. Moot in the end, because Brian wants the bed strip and the back face flat ("do not add a round over to the back of the part. Use this flat surface to print the part vertically"), which is the better print anyway: the first layer is a full flat strip.
- **"The back" has two readings for a standing part.** Brian's sentence can mean the back face stays square and the part prints standing as before (built), or that the part should lie on its flat back (a 4-per-plate layout, three plates). The build took the reading that keeps every earlier decision and asked; write the alternative in the brief so the swap is one constant if he meant the other. Brian confirmed the first reading the next day ("Keep standing", 2026-11-10), so the question cost one line in the brief and nothing in the pipeline.
- The 3MF builder ran while the STL kept changing. Because it reads the constants and the STL at run time, re-running it on the final hook took 12 s plus the proof slice. Keep print-file builders parameter-free and re-runnable; never bake geometry numbers into them.

## Numbers

- Hook 4 x 4 x 3/8 in (tab 1-3/4 wide and 4 tall on the board so five butted hooks fill the 20 in hang, jaw 2-1/4 past the edge). Slot 7/8 wide leaning 35 degrees from vertical away from the wood, rounded bottom R 7/16 with its low point 5/8 up; ramp leaves the tab edge 1-7/8 up; lip top 1-7/8 sloping to a 2-1/4 tip, lip 0.58 at its thinnest; 1/8 web keeps the rod off the wood. Lift to leave 1.57 in for 1/4, 3/8 and 5/8 blanks (plain deep hook 1.00; the wrong-way angled floor 1.24). Screws #8 x 1, 1 in from the board edge, 9/16 and 3-7/16 up from the hook bottom, 5 mm holes with a 10 mm 90 degree countersink.
- PETG, 2 walls, 15% gyroid, 0.30 mm layers, 0.6 nozzle. Standing: 33.2 g / 1 h 19 for one bare hook alone; `rod-hook.3mf` with all ten standing in a row on one plate: 372.8 g, 8 h 10, 37.3 g and 49 min per hook with pads. Flat, for the record: 30.0 g / 39 min per hook, three plates (4, 4, 2) 343 g / 7 h 48. Standing costs about 9% more plastic and 22 minutes, and saves two plate changes. Pads: 0.875 to 0.89 plastic per volume vs 0.14 plain, all 20.
- Butt hook (2026-11-09): 4-1/2 x 4 x 3/8, jaw 2-3/4 past the edge, slot 2 in leaning 10 degrees, rounded bottom R 1 with its low point 5/16 up, ramp off the tab edge at 1-5/8, lip top 1-5/8 sloping 1/8 to a 1-3/4 tip, lip 0.55 at its thinnest; a 1-7/8 grip seats with its centre 1.25 up and 1.19 out, 1/16 clear of each wall; 2-1/4 in between the lip tip and the hook stacked above (Brian asked for 2); lift to leave 1.405 in for 1-1/2 to 1-7/8 grips; overhang standing 10 degrees; 58.2 cm3 (tip hook 63.8) with the roundover; bare 30.9 g / 1 h 19 (tip 33.2 g / 1 h 18). Roundover 1/32 on the front face's outline and the countersink rims; back face and bottom strip square. `rod-hook.3mf` (2026-11-09, final): ONE plate, five pairs in one row at 23 mm pitch, tip hooks at the back, butt hooks in front: 359.2 g / 7 h 56 (35.9 g, 48 min per hook with pads), pads 0.873 to 0.89 vs 0.14, round trip keeps the plate. For the record, the two-plate version of the same day (ten of each: tip 372.8 g / 8 h 10, butt 346.9 g / 7 h 46) was built before Brian said "there should only be 5 pairs"; `PLATES = {"tip": 1, "butt": 2}` brings it back.
- 1.5 in hook (2026-11-11, Brian: "Let's create a 1.5 in version ... incorporate the angled slot like in the original design"): 4-5/8 x 4 x 3/8, jaw 2-7/8 past the edge, slot 1-1/2 leaning 35 degrees (the tip hook's lean, low end away from the wood), rounded bottom R 3/4 with its low point 5/16 up, lip top 1-7/8 sloping 3/8 to a 2-1/4 tip, lip 0.52 at its thinnest; 1-3/4 in between the lip tip and the hook above; a 1-3/8 grip seats 1.00 up and 1.61 out; lift to leave 1.873 in (tip 1.571, 2 in 1.405); overhang 35; 63.5 cm3; bare 33.3 g / 1 h 19. `rod-hook.3mf` with all three, five of each on ONE plate: 545.9 g / 12 h 00 (36.4 g, 48 min per hook), 30 pads 0.873 to 0.89 vs 0.14.
- **The lean is bought with slot width.** In the 4 in pitch the 2 in hook could only lean 10 degrees because its lip had to drop to let a 1-7/8 grip pass. A 1-1/2 in slot for grips to 1-3/8 keeps the tip hook's lip (tip at 2-1/4, 1-3/4 gap) and so the whole 35 degrees. Same inequality, written first, answered "can it have the original angle" before any drawing.
- **Floor against lift, bounded by the fillet assert.** With the lean and lip fixed, lowering the floor is the only lever on hold: 1/2 floor 1.675 in of lift, 7/16 1.749, 3/8 1.811, 5/16 1.873 (built). The tip hook's 5/8 floor and a lower, steeper lip both fail "fillet 0.125 too big" for this slot. A ten-line candidate table over the real `Hook` class settled it in one run.
- **Put neighbours of the OTHER kinds into the hold check.** Two grip hooks will share a board, so each hook's escape lift is also computed with every other variant butted above and below (`escape_lift_mixed_in`). No number moved, which is the proof that any order works.
- **Labels that reach file and plate names must be slash-free.** The hook is "1.5 in" in names and "1-1/2 in" in dimensions; a `label` on the `Hook` object feeds the reports, the plate name, the drawings and the page, so Brian's own words ("the 2 in version") appear everywhere and "butt" stays an internal key.
- **Fifteen standing hooks fit one plate only edge to edge.** Two hooks end to end (101.6 + 13.5 + 117.5 mm) span x 11.7 to 244.3, inside the 5 mm margin but across the 20.5 mm left strip that the X2D's second nozzle cannot reach. The slice is clean on extruder 1 and refused on extruder 2 (CLI return -66), so a single-material plate may use the strip; read `extruder_printable_area` before promising a full-width layout for a two-material job.
- **Max-effort subagents are slow on small jobs.** The print-file agent took 32 minutes and the render agent 17 for work whose tools run in about three; Brian asked whether something was hung. For a known pipeline with a layout already specified, edit the script directly or brief the agent to run first and explore after.
- Board layout drawn: five hooks butted, the top one at the joist bottom, bottoms at 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 in below the joist on the 27 in board (rods about 3-3/16 down, then every 4; grip centres about 2-3/4 down on the butt board); the same drilling on both boards; reels on and in line would want 5 in, i.e. four hooks with gaps.
- Brian sizes by the board: "5 holders should fit in the 20 in, no space between each hook" fixed the hook height at hang / count. Ask for the count and the hang before fixing a tab height; the pitch falls out of it.
- **A second size of the same hook is a height budget before it is a shape.** Brian asked (2026-11-09) for a butt-end version with a 2 in opening for the grip, in the same 4 in pitch. The grip has to pass between the lip tip and the hook butted above, so tip height + grip + clearance <= 4 fixed the lip tip at 1-3/4 before any drawing; the floor (5/16), the lean (10 degrees, down from 35) and the lip slope (1/8) then divided what was left. Writing the budget as one inequality first saved the cycle the tip hook's lean would have cost (35 degrees with a 2 in slot wants about 7 in of hook). The hold is all lift on this one (1.405 in; the hood is gone), which is what a near-vertical 2 in slot can offer in 4 in. Trap found by the fillet assert, not by eye: a 3/8 floor at 10 degrees left the lip's straight inner face shorter than the R 1/8 corner's tangent length; 5/16 fixed it and gained 1/16 of hold.
- **Put the hooks above and below into the kinematics.** The pose-space check had only the hook itself as an obstacle; for the tip hook that was fine by inspection (1-1/8 to spare over the lip tip), for a 2 in grip the hook butted above is the binding constraint. `obstacles()` now unions the hook, its copies shifted by plus and minus H, and the board, so `connected()` proves the pass under the neighbour and the lift is measured against it. The tip hook's numbers did not change, which is the regression test.
- **Variants want an object from day one.** The hook's numbers were module globals (`SLOT_W`, `CANT`, ...) read by three pipeline scripts; the second version turned them into a `Hook` class with `TIP` and `BUTT` instances and broke every `rh.CANT` downstream. When a part has even one plausible second size, hold its numbers in an object and have the pipeline take the variant from the start.
- **Standing prints need their own checks.** Brian asked for the hooks printed vertically so a plate holds many more. A 4 x 4 x 3/8 plate on its 4 in edge has a 101.6 x 9.5 mm footprint, so ten fit one plate. What changed with the orientation: every downward-facing surface became an overhang question (the slot walls lean 35 degrees, fine; the R 1/16 bottom-corner rounds were a 43 degree overhang at the bed and went to square corners), the screw holes became horizontal 5 mm bridges, and the lip's bending loads moved onto the layer bond. `overhang_report()` in `rod_hook.py` reads the exported STL's face normals, skips the bed face and the holes, and asserts the steepest surface against 45 degrees. Export two STLs when the print frame differs from the model frame: the renders keep the model frame, the slicer gets the print frame, and the 3MF builder must know which one it has (pad axes follow the holes).

## Still to learn (after the print)

- Does a 3/8 in PETG hook on two #8 screws shrug off a yank on a snagged rod? Expectation: yes by a wide margin.
- Does the up-the-ramp-then-out motion (1.57 in of lift) feel right overhead, or is it fiddly with a reel on?
- Rod blank diameters at the tip-hook positions (the 7/8 pocket), and the fattest grip at the butt hook: the 2 in opening leaves 1/16 per side on a 1-7/8 grip, and the 2-1/4 in gap over the lip tip is what a grip passes through overhead.
- The grips the 1.5 in hook is for (assumed about 1 to 1-3/8 in, unmeasured), whether its 1.87 in of lift is too much travel overhead, and the mix of 2 in and 1.5 in hooks Brian actually mounts.
- Whether the two back-row hooks, 13.5 mm apart end to end at the bed edges, print as cleanly as the centred ones.
- Whether the 1/32 roundover reads as "very slight" in the hand on a 0.6 nozzle at 0.3 mm layers (it spans under three layers on the top edges and prints as a vertical round on the sides).
- Whether a 1-7/8 grip drops into a 2 in slot cleanly in the dark, or whether the opening wants 2-1/8 (one constant, `slot_w`, at the cost of 1/8 of lip reach).
