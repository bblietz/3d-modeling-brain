# Guitar cabinet corner joinery survey

Research run for the Plan 2 brainstorm ([[2026-09-10-plan-2-generator-design]]) after Brian ruled "finger joints only" and asked whether any alternative is equally good. Corner joint means the four front-to-back edges where top, bottom, and sides meet. Maker statements were preferred over dealer copy and forums; entries marked unverified rest on a dealer or reseller page.

## Is the finger joint the consensus?

No. It is the plurality among plywood cabs and the default for vintage-correct reproduction (eleven verified), not a consensus. Mesa/Boogie, the largest high-end cab maker, uses a glued and nailed rabbet; Morgan, Friedman, and Tone King publish tongue and groove; 3rd Power dado; Sourmash dado plus dowels. The most prestigious solid-wood cabs (Carr, Benson, Milkman's walnut customs) and two plywood specialists (Emperor, Atlas) use dovetails. Premier Guitar's summary fits: finger joints are sought after "because that's how Leo did it", dovetails are "the strongest of all", and joint type has "virtually no effect on the sound" unless the corner flexes.

## Alternatives and verdicts

- **Through dovetail.** Emperor, Carr, Benson, Atlas (half-blind), Milkman. Corner-bending tests (Wandel, woodgears.ca) put box joints and dovetails within noise; a dovetail cannot beat an ideal narrow-finger box joint because the tail necks down, but it adds a one-direction mechanical lock at equal glue area. Slowest setup, needs a wide jig for 280 mm panels. Shows end grain in the traditional show-wood rhythm. Verdict: genuinely equal, the only true peer for the hardwood line. Adopted as the hardwood option.
- **Lock miter.** No commercial cab builder verified. Large glue area, self-aligning, no fingers against racking, fragile knife edge, and a solid-wood miter opens seasonally, which on a cab is an air leak and a rattle path. Best looking on bare hardwood (grain wraps the corner). Verdict: worse structurally, not equal.
- **Splined or keyed miter.** No cab builder verified. Splines restore long-grain glue plus a lock but do not cure the miter's movement problem. Verdict: worse than a box joint; the keys are a design feature, not a structural equal.
- **Rabbet with dowels.** Mesa/Boogie and Sourmash prove it road-worthy at scale. Less long-grain glue area than a box joint; dowels add pull-apart resistance. Verdict: adequate under tolex, a step down on show wood.
- **Corner posts or battens.** Celestion's own build guide specifies butt joints screwed and glued with reinforcing battens. Costs internal volume and weight, plain corner. Verdict: fine for tolex, weakest-looking on hardwood. Dropped from the hardwood line on 2026-09-10.

## Finger conventions

- **Width.** Production shops cut 1/4 in on 5/8 and 3/4 in stock (Guitar Cabinets Direct, Mojotone). DIY plan sets run 1/2 in. Fine Woodworking: fingers half the stock thickness. No measured vintage Marshall or Fender finger width was found. Brian's choice for the generator: half the panel thickness (9 mm on 18 mm birch, 9.5 mm on 19 mm hardwood).
- **Layout.** Both ends get a full finger; nobody endorses a partial edge finger. Woodcraft: plan the panel so two opposing sides start and end with a full finger; Leigh sizes the panel to the finger. Equivalent for a fixed depth: divide by an odd count.
- **Front edge.** Only forum guidance exists (The Amp Garage): the long pieces start with a finger at the front edge, the short pieces with a gap, so the front corner lands in solid material. Adopted: top and bottom carry the front full finger.
- **Wood movement.** The joint is safe at 280 mm, but all four hardwood panels must run grain around the box (front to back on every panel) so every panel moves along the depth axis together. Expect roughly 2.6 mm (cherry), 2.8 (sapele), 2.9 (walnut), 3.7 (hard maple) of depth change per 4-point moisture swing flatsawn, about half quartersawn. Do not glue a plywood baffle or back into a full-length dado across the depth direction; glue the front portion and let the rest float.
