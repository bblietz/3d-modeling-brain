# Your MaximoCabs Tolex 1x12

Prepared for Pat Player.

## 1. Your rig and goals

<!-- slot: rig_and_goals -->
You play a 1965 Fender Deluxe Reverb reissue, roots and alt-country, at low-volume gigs in small clubs of 150 people at most. In your words: "Want a tight low end and rolled highs." This cabinet hangs on the Deluxe's extension speaker jack and works alongside the combo's own speaker, on the floor beside it. With the combo's 8 ohm speaker and this 16 ohm cabinet in parallel, the amp sees a combined load of about 5.3 ohm (8 || 16) on its 8 ohm output: a 2:1 mismatch that a tube amp of this kind tolerates, and one we call out here so it is a choice, not a surprise.
<!-- /slot -->

## 2. The recommended cabinet

- Cabinet: Tolex 1x12, closed-back, ported
- Configuration: 1x12
- Speaker: Celestion G12H Anniversary
- Impedance and wiring: Single driver, 16 ohm
- External size, width x height x depth: 17-7/8 x 16-5/16 x 9-15/16 in
- Estimated weight, loaded: 31.2 lb (14.2 kg)

<!-- slot: why_this_cabinet -->
- Speaker: the Celestion G12H Anniversary at 16 ohm is your pick, and it fits the brief: a ceramic-magnet driver with strong low-mids and a controlled top that keeps a bright blackface amp from turning thin, with the handling for a 22 W combo that is sharing its output with its own speaker.
- Back type: closed and ported, because a tight low end and a cabinet that projects to the microphone both call for a closed box; the port is tuned below the speaker's own resonance so it adds weight, not boom.
- Size: smaller than our standard 20 x 18 x 11 in box on purpose. The internal volume is designed around this speaker for a tight, fast low end rather than a big one, and it is voiced for the floor, where the room already adds bass.
<!-- /slot -->

## 3. What it is designed to do

<!-- slot: designed_to_do -->
This cabinet is designed for a tight, dry low end under a blackface combo on a club floor, with the port adding low-mid weight rather than boom, so the bass stays clear at low volume in a small room. It is designed to round off the Deluxe Reverb's brightness rather than add chime, which is where the G12H's top end and a closed back both pull. It is designed to be mic'd: a closed back throws forward and keeps the guitar out of the vocal mic. It is designed to sit on the floor beside the combo, share the amp's extension jack with the combo's own speaker, and carry in one hand by the strap handle.
<!-- /slot -->

## 4. Alternatives considered

<!-- slot: alternatives -->
- Eminence Cannabis Rex (50 W): the blackface roots speaker, neutral mids and a smooth top with more headroom; the same tight box could be voiced around it if you want more clean headroom than the G12H offers.
- Jensen C12N (50 W): the classic Deluxe Reverb driver, but it wants an open back to sound its best, which means a wider, less tight low end than you asked for.
- Celestion G12-65 Heritage (65 W): tight, smooth, and moderate on paper with more headroom, but a Marshall-family voice that leans away from a Fender rig; our data for it is thinner, so its box would be sized by rule of thumb.
<!-- /slot -->
