# Your MaximoCabs Tolex 1x12

Prepared for Pat Player.

## 1. Your rig and goals

<!-- slot: rig_and_goals -->
You play a 1965 Fender Deluxe Reverb reissue, roots and alt-country, at low-volume gigs in small clubs of 150 people at most. In your words: "Want a tight low end and rolled highs." This cabinet hangs on the Deluxe's extension speaker jack and works alongside the combo's own speaker, on the floor beside it. With the combo's 8 ohm speaker and this 8 ohm cabinet in parallel, the amp sees a combined load of about 4 ohm (8 || 8) on its 8 ohm output: a 2:1 mismatch that a tube amp of this kind tolerates, the same load any 8 ohm extension cabinet puts on that jack, and one we call out here so it is a choice, not a surprise.
<!-- /slot -->

## 2. The recommended cabinet

- Cabinet: Tolex 1x12, closed-back, ported
- Configuration: 1x12
- Speaker: Eminence Cannabis Rex 12
- Impedance and wiring: Single driver, 8 ohm
- External size, width x height x depth: 18.7 x 18.2 x 9.9 in (475 x 463 x 252 mm)
- Estimated weight, loaded: 32.0 lb (14.5 kg)

<!-- slot: why_this_cabinet -->
- Speaker: the Eminence Cannabis Rex at 8 ohm is your pick, and it is the one we would have reached for: a hemp-cone driver with neutral mids and a smooth, rounded top that takes the edge off a bright blackface amp without going dull, with 50 W of handling for a 22 W combo that is sharing its output with its own speaker.
- Back type: closed and ported, because a tight low end and a cabinet that projects to the microphone both call for a closed box. The port is a front slot along the bottom of the baffle, the same kind of vent the classic ported guitar cabinets use, chosen over a rear tube because it fits this box without compromise and keeps the back panel clean.
- Size: a little smaller than our standard 20 x 18 x 11 in box on purpose. The internal volume is designed around this speaker for a tight, fast low end rather than a big one, and it is voiced for the floor, where the room already adds bass.
<!-- /slot -->

## 3. What it is designed to do

<!-- slot: designed_to_do -->
This cabinet is designed for a tight, dry low end under a blackface combo on a club floor, with the front slot adding low-mid weight rather than boom, so the bass stays clear at low volume in a small room. It is designed to round off the Deluxe Reverb's brightness rather than add chime, which is where the Cannabis Rex's hemp cone and a closed back both pull. It is designed to be mic'd: a closed back throws forward and keeps the guitar out of the vocal mic, and the slot vents forward with it. It is designed to sit on the floor beside the combo, share the amp's extension jack with the combo's own speaker, and carry in one hand by the strap handle.
<!-- /slot -->

## 4. Alternatives considered

<!-- slot: alternatives -->
- Jensen C12N (50 W): the classic Deluxe Reverb driver with the same neutral mids, but it wants an open back to sound its best, which means a wider, less tight low end than you asked for.
- Eminence Red White and Blues (120 W): tight and neutral with far more headroom, but a brighter top than "rolled highs" asks for; a better fit for a cleaner, louder rig.
- Celestion G12-65 Heritage (65 W): tight, smooth, and moderate on paper, but a Marshall-family voice that leans away from a Fender rig; our data for it is thinner, so its box would be sized by rule of thumb.
- A rear round port instead of the front slot: it fits this box only at its shortest length, which pulls the tuning off target; the slot holds the tuning and stays out of the way of the jack plate.
<!-- /slot -->
