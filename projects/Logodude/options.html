Logo Dude, three ways to print it

Every render below is built from a 1:1 trace of your logo-dude.png, brush streaks included; nothing is redrawn. Shown at 100 mm wide, white base with the ink raised 1 mm in black (two AMS colors).

Style

A · Plaque

Rounded plaque

The drawing framed on a rounded square with a 7 mm border. Wall art, a desk sign, or a badge.

114 × 111 × 4 mm
B · Die-cut

Die-cut outline

The backer follows the drawing with a 3.5 mm margin, like a die-cut sticker. Good as a magnet, a wall piece, or a cab badge.

107 × 104 × 4 mm
C · Desk stand

Standing figure

The die-cut shape, 4 mm thick, slotted into a small base so it stands on a desk or shelf. Two parts.

107 × 104 figure, 63 × 24 mm base

Notes

Brush streaks. The dry-brush gaps in the hair are part of the trace. Below about 125 mm they are thinner than a nozzle line, so the slicer fills them in and the hair prints as solid strokes. That matches how the Sharks tags handled hairlines; nothing gets widened or redrawn.

Raised or flush. Raised ink (shown) gives a tactile edge you can feel. A flush inlay, black sunk level with the white, prints a flat ironed top that looks like ink on paper. Same trace either way.

Colors. Two by default: white base, black ink. Any AMS colors work, up to four.
