Which bulky leg/corner treatment?

Drawer Bench, 36" W x 24" D x 20" H starting size. Maple butcherblock top, soft maple legs and drawer fronts, 3/4" ply sides/back. Each drawing is to scale: front elevation (left) and side elevation (right). Click the one that feels right; comment in the terminal.

A

Proud corner posts (post & panel)

Four solid maple posts (~3" square) run floor to top and ARE the structure; plywood panels and the face frame join into them. Drawer fronts run between the posts (~29" wide). Classic mission/shaker bench. Bulkiest, most "legs" read; front is framed by visible maple posts.

B

Flush wrapped corners (chunky feet)

The plywood case is the structure; L-shaped soft maple wraps glued over each corner read as solid ~3" posts from the side, and continue ~3" below the case as chunky feet. Drawer fronts run the FULL 36" width, edge to edge. Modern but still heavy-looking; front is all continuous maple.

C

Solid maple plinth base

No legs: the case sits on a heavy solid maple plinth frame (~4" tall), set back so the bench appears to float slightly. Bulk lives at the base instead of the corners. Full-width drawer fronts, most grounded look, easiest to build; reads more "cabinet" than "bench on legs".

Notes

A gives ~29" wide drawers; B and C give the full ~36". A is the most traditional joinery (panels housed in posts); B gets the same look from the outside with simpler casework. Hybrids are possible, for example B's wrapped corners plus a plinth. After this we'll pick post/foot thickness and reveal details.
