Post edges and feet?

3" square posts locked. Now the detail treatment. Each card shows the post cross-section (large scale) and the bottom ~12" of a post at the floor. This is mostly about look, but edges matter on a bench: calves and shins touch it.

A

Crisp square

No treatment: sharp arrises, maximum timber mass. Bulkiest read, but edges will dent and feel hard against legs.

B

3/8" chamfer (recommended)

All four vertical edges chamfered 3/8", full length. Keeps the timber-frame bulk, catches light as a facet, friendly to shins, hides wear. The classic move on chunky legs.

C

Tapered foot

Square post, but the two inside faces taper over the bottom 4" (3" down to ~2-1/4" at the floor). Lightens the stance and makes it look deliberate, at the cost of some bulk.

D

Chamfer + tapered foot

Both details together: faceted edges plus a lighter stance. The most "furniture maker" look, least raw-timber.

Notes

Recommendation: B. You like bulky; the chamfer keeps every bit of the 3" mass while making the edges livable, and the taper (C/D) works against bulk. Chamfer size is tweakable later (1/4" subtle to 1/2" bold). Next question: how far the drawer fronts sit back from the post faces, then drawer count and layout.
