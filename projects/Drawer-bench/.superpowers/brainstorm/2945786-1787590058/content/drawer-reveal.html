How far back do the drawer fronts sit?

Chamfered 3" posts locked. The drawer fronts are a continuous maple plane between the posts; this question is where that plane sits relative to the post faces. Each card: plan view from above (large scale) and a crop of the front corner showing the resulting shadow line.

A

Flush

Drawer faces dead level with the post faces: one continuous plane, posts read as thin maple stripes. Sleekest, but demands perfect co-planar alignment and stays honest only if nothing moves. Least "framed" look.

B

1/4" setback (recommended)

Fronts sit 1/4" behind the post faces: a crisp shadow line frames the drawer plane, and small alignment errors disappear. The posts clearly read as structure holding a maple panel. Standard furniture move.

C

3/4" deep reveal

Fronts recessed a full 3/4": deep shadow, posts strongly dominant, the drawer bank looks set into a timber frame. Dramatic, but the top overhang plus deep reveal starts to shade the drawer faces, and dust ledges form on the face frame edges.

Notes

Recommendation: B. Flush (A) is high-risk to keep perfect across seasons; C is handsome but fussy in use. Assumption I'm carrying unless you object: the plywood side and back panels sit set back ~1/2" from the post faces (classic frame-and-panel shadow) with grain running consistently.
