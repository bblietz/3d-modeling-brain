How beefy is the butcherblock top?

Last big look question. Thickness and overhang past the posts, drawn to scale on the locked design (3" chamfered posts, graduated drawers). Thickness also decides buy vs glue-up.

A

1-1/2" thick, 3/4" overhang

Standard counter stock: cheapest and easiest to buy off the shelf (most prefab maple butcherblock is 1-1/2"). Cleanest, most cabinet-like look; the top reads as a lid.

B

1-3/4" thick, 1-1/4" overhang

Workbench-grade prefab (John Boos class) or an easy glue-up. Noticeably more slab presence with a friendlier shadow for knees when sitting. Middle path.

C

2-1/4" thick, 1-1/2" overhang

True slab: the top matches the posts for mass, unmistakably bulky. Usually means gluing up your own from 8/4 maple (buying this thick gets expensive fast) and it adds real weight (~15 lb over A).

Notes

Recommendation: C if you're up for the glue-up, since it completes the bulky brief; B if you'd rather buy a prefab top and trim it. Either way the top gets attached with slotted screws or figure-8 fasteners: a 24" deep maple slab moves ~1/8-1/4" across the seasons and must not be rigidly pinned to the case.
