# Freestanding alcove bench

The second design for the 66 x 28 in former wet-bar alcove. On 2026-10-26 the client asked for the bench to be free standing and high enough for their Roomba to get underneath it. This project is that design; the built-in design in [[Built-in-bench]] (`projects/Built-in-bench/`, shop page https://claude.ai/artifact/YQFcx4g5x64K83yhDHvGMf, Version 18) is untouched in case they go back. Designed with the [[furniture]] skill.

## What carried over from the built-in (Brian's locked picks)

Wood top at 14 in, seat 17 in with the 3 in cushion (kept for Maizy, the senior dog). Full-overlay-style fronts built as a 1-1/2 in maple frame around a 1/2 in paint-grade panel inset 1/4 in on the tongue-and-groove bit set, all painted white. 8 in brass bar pulls. 3/4 in overhang with a 1/8 roundover. Solid glued-up maple top with its front edge 3 in back from the pilaster faces. One full-depth cushion. 21 in Blum TANDEM 563H undermounts with every Blum rule from the built-in brief. Drawer boxes of 1/2 in 9-ply Baltic birch (no groove in the back, bottom butts it, glued and, since 2026-11-21, screwed through the back with four #6 x 1 at 3/4 in up, Brian: "add the screws"). Osmo Natural on the bare maple. The picture-only shelves and dropped soffit stay in the room picture.

## How it stands: the drawer bench's legs (Brian, 2026-10-26)

My first version put the case on four short legs under a box (a C-frame of rails, or blades, or gables); Brian: "legs are all wrong, look at the legs for the other bench we designed and let's start there, show 5 options, visual companion". Five posts were drawn from the drawer bench's construction ([[drawer-bench]]: proud solid maple corner posts floor to the underside of the top, 3/8 chamfer on the four vertical edges, panels housed in stopped grooves 1/2 in behind the post faces, fronts between the posts 1/4 in behind their faces) and shown in the superpowers visual companion (`leg_options.py`, images in `images/legs/`): A 2-1/2 in posts, B 3 in as the drawer bench, C 3-1/2 in, D 3 in with the chamfer stopped over a square foot, E 3 in with a tapered foot. Brian picked A. The base options page (https://claude.ai/artifact/QLCHadrNJZDeDjqka9yond, Version 2) records the superseded bases and the pick.

- **Posts**: 2-1/2 in square soft maple, each four 3/4 layers of 1x6 (2-5/8 x 14-1/4 blanks) face-glued to 3 in and planed square, glue lines turned to the side faces (Brian 2026-11-20: no 12/4 to be had; hollow mitered posts with walnut splines were drawn and dropped as too error-prone, pictures in `images/options/post-*.jpg`), floor to 13-1/4, 3/8 chamfer full length, outer faces 62-1/2 apart, straight to the floor, no grooves since 2026-11-20 (the panels butt them, see Case joints); no notch, they stand 2 in off the back wall, 3/4 in clear of the 1-1/4 in baseboard-and-shoe estimate (Brian 2026-11-01, replacing the 1-1/2 x 4-1/2 notch of the first version). Front posts take the Blum runner front screws on their inner faces (1-1/4 and 2-5/16 behind the front face, #6 x 5/8).
- **Case joints** (Brian 2026-11-20: "i have a kreg, switch all the case joints"): glue and Kreg pocket screws everywhere, replacing the drawer bench's stopped grooves, notched panel corners, the bottom's rabbets and the partition dado. The ends (3/4 paint-grade birch ply, show faces painted white, bottom edges banded) and the back (3/4 birch ply now, was 1/2: pocket screws hold poorly in 1/2 and the back is the shear panel) are plain rectangles butting the posts with their outer faces 1/2 in behind the post faces (a 1/2 in spacer block sets it), two pockets on the inside face at each vertical edge, 1-1/4 fine-thread screws into the maple. The back's pockets sit 3 and 5-1/4 in up, above the Blum rear brackets and below the cleat; the brackets screw to its inside face with #6 x 1/2. The model asserts every butt joint and keeps each pocket's recess clear of the bottom, the top's attachment pockets, the runners, the brackets and the cleat.
- **Case bottom** 3/4 birch ply butting the ends and the back, underside flush with their bottom edges, all four corners notched around the posts, glued and pocket-screwed from underneath (three pockets along each end edge, six along the rear edge, 1-1/4 coarse thread). **Partition** stands on the bottom (three #8 x 2 screws up through the bottom into its edge) and butts the back (two pockets at 2-1/2 and 4-1/4 in up, above the runners), front edge on the fronts' back-face line; the partition-side runners mount to it the frameless way. **Cleat** 3/4 x 2-1/2 poplar on the back's inside face at the top with four figure-8 recesses for the top.
- **Fronts** between the front posts: 28-9/16 x 8-1/8, 1/8 reveals to the posts, between and under the top, faces 1/4 behind the post faces, bottoms flush with the case underside at 5 in.
- **Top** 65-1/2 x 24-3/4 x 3/4, 1/4 in off the back wall and off each side wall; past the posts 1-1/2 at the ends, 3/4 at the front and 1-3/4 at the back. Overall 65-1/2 x 24-3/4 x 17 with the cushion. (Version 1 was 64 wide with 1 in of air at the ends and flush with the posts at the back.)
- **Drawers**: boxes 27-11/16 x 6-1/2 x 18 (5-17/32 inside), the 5 in of clear floor coming out of the height at the fixed 17 in seat. Inside depth from the fronts' back faces to the back's inner face is 510.1 mm (516.1 with the 1/2 back) against Blum's 481 for the 18 in class on the partition side; on the post side the 471 mm runner body (measured on the drawer bench's 563H4570B) ends 38.6 mm in front of the back, inside the rear bracket's 6 to 48 mm.

Why the rear posts are 2 in off the wall and the runners 18 in (2026-11-01): Brian wanted the baseboard notch gone and the top 1/4 in from the walls all round. Posts clear of the baseboard cost the case 1-3/4 in of depth, which the 21 in runners could not spare (557 mm minimum against 560.6), so Brian picked 18 in runners over pulling the case 1-1/2 in forward or the baseboard out. The 18 in runner then set the post position from the other side: the Blum rear bracket wants the runner's tail within 48 mm of the back, and posts at 1-3/4 in off the wall left 51 mm, so they stand at 2 in (44.6 mm, and 3/4 in to spare over the unmeasured baseboard). The 1/2 in back stays.

## Materials (Brian, 2026-11-20)

"No hard maple, all soft": the top, cleat and posts are soft maple. "I can't source 12/4 maple, so the legs will be made from 1x6": each post is four 3/4 layers glued and planed to 2-1/2. "The cabinet will be painted white, why maple plywood?" and "the drawer faces were supposed to be made with poplar frame and plywood face": the frames are poplar and the panels paint-grade plywood (no MDF), and the case plywood is paint-grade birch, the paint-grade swap the built-in brief already listed. The cleat is poplar too, cut from the poplar board with the frames (Brian 2026-11-20), which saves a maple board.

## Clear floor, 5 in

Roomba heights: j7 and Combo j7+ 3.4 in, i7 and the 600/900 series 3.6 in, the 2025 lidar models (Plus 405/505) about 3.3 to 3.9 in, the Max 705 fits a 4 in gap, other lidar robots 3.8 to 4.0 in. Five inches leaves an inch over the tallest and room for a rug under the bench. Sources: https://www.irobot.com/en_US/roomba-j7-robot-vacuum/J715020.html, https://irobotthailand.com/en/shop/irobot-roomba-i7/, https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/vacuum-cleaners/irobot-roomba-max-705/m417680/, https://www.sylvane.com/collections/robotic-vacuum-cleaners/products/irobot-roomba-plus-415-combo-vacuum-mop-autowash-dock.

## Model and pipeline

`freestanding_bench.py` (build123d, mm inside, inch constants, same axes and conventions as `built_in_bench.py`): PARTS/INST/HW, asserts for one solid per part, no overlaps, the post volumes against the box minus chamfers, the panels clear of the chamfers, every case butt joint, the pocket-hole recesses clear of the runners, brackets, cleat and each other, the bottom's notches clear of the posts, the baseboard envelope clear of everything, the clear floor (nothing but the posts below 5 in), the Blum rules (box height, inside depth, bracket range at 533 and 547, runner screws inside the post, hook bore and notch probes). `EXPORT=1` writes `cutlist.md`/`.csv` (15 part rows, hardware with the rear brackets, banding, cushion) and `freestanding_bench.step`.

`hero_shot.py` (viewer), `assembly_shots.py` (exploded view, 10 steps: posts, end assemblies, back and cleat, bottom, partition, slides and brackets, boxes, fronts and pulls, top, cushion; end, drawer and front explosions), `part_drawings.py` (sheets in `images/parts/`, every header checked against the cut list), `make_cutlist_page.py` (shop page), `make_pdf.py`, `photo_match.py` (the built-in's camera match on this model; `check` measures the brass pulls and the front posts' maple columns), `render_in_room.py` (jobs guided, styled, dogs, soffit-paint), `leg_options.py` (the five post options for the companion).
