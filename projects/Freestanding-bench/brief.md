# Freestanding alcove bench

The second design for the 66 x 28 in former wet-bar alcove. On 2026-10-26 the client asked for the bench to be free standing and high enough for their Roomba to get underneath it. This project is that design; the built-in design in [[Built-in-bench]] (`projects/Built-in-bench/`, shop page https://claude.ai/artifact/YQFcx4g5x64K83yhDHvGMf, Version 18) is untouched in case they go back. Designed with the [[furniture]] skill.

## What carried over from the built-in (Brian's locked picks)

Wood top at 14 in, seat 17 in with the 3 in cushion (kept for Maizy, the senior dog). Full-overlay-style fronts built as a 1-1/2 in maple frame around a 1/2 in paint-grade panel inset 1/4 in on the tongue-and-groove bit set, all painted white. 8 in brass bar pulls. 3/4 in overhang with a 1/8 roundover. Solid glued-up maple top with its front edge 3 in back from the pilaster faces. One full-depth cushion. 21 in Blum TANDEM 563H undermounts with every Blum rule from the built-in brief. Drawer boxes of 1/2 in 9-ply Baltic birch (no groove in the back, bottom butts it). Osmo Natural on the bare maple. The picture-only shelves and dropped soffit stay in the room picture.

## How it stands: the drawer bench's legs (Brian, 2026-10-26)

My first version put the case on four short legs under a box (a C-frame of rails, or blades, or gables); Brian: "legs are all wrong, look at the legs for the other bench we designed and let's start there, show 5 options, visual companion". Five posts were drawn from the drawer bench's construction ([[drawer-bench]]: proud solid maple corner posts floor to the underside of the top, 3/8 chamfer on the four vertical edges, panels housed in stopped grooves 1/2 in behind the post faces, fronts between the posts 1/4 in behind their faces) and shown in the superpowers visual companion (`leg_options.py`, images in `images/legs/`): A 2-1/2 in posts, B 3 in as the drawer bench, C 3-1/2 in, D 3 in with the chamfer stopped over a square foot, E 3 in with a tapered foot. Brian picked A. The base options page (https://claude.ai/artifact/QLCHadrNJZDeDjqka9yond, Version 2) records the superseded bases and the pick.

- **Posts**: 2-1/2 in square soft maple (12/4 stock or an 8/4 plus 4/4 glue-up), floor to 13-1/4, 3/8 chamfer full length cut before the grooves, outer faces 62-1/2 apart, straight to the floor. End-panel groove 3/4 ply wide x 3/8 deep on the face toward the other post of that end, 1/2 in from the outer face, from 5-3/4 up and out the top (the groove end hides behind the panel's un-housed bottom). Rear posts also carry the back's groove on their inner face, 1/2 in from the rear face; no notch, they stand 2 in off the back wall, 3/4 in clear of the 1-1/4 in baseboard-and-shoe estimate (Brian 2026-11-01, replacing the 1-1/2 x 4-1/2 notch of the first version). Front posts take the Blum runner front screws on their inner faces (1-1/4 and 2-5/16 behind the front face, #6 x 5/8).
- **Panels**: ends 3/4 maple ply (show faces, painted white, bottom edges banded), back 1/2 maple ply, both housed 3/8 in the posts with their outer faces 1/2 in behind the post faces, bottom corners notched 3/8 x 1-1/2 so the tongue starts 1-1/2 in up and the bottom butts the post faces, a 1/4 x 3/4 rabbet along the bottom inside edge for the case bottom. The back is glued in and is the shear panel; the Blum rear brackets screw to it with #6 x 1/2.
- **Case bottom** 3/4 ply in those rabbets, all four corners notched around the posts (1-17/32 across, 1-17/32 front and 1-25/32 rear), centered partition dado. **Partition** against the back with its front edge on the fronts' back-face line; the partition-side runners mount to it the frameless way. **Cleat** 3/4 x 2-1/2 soft maple on the back's inside face at the top with four figure-8 recesses for the top.
- **Fronts** between the front posts: 28-9/16 x 8-1/8, 1/8 reveals to the posts, between and under the top, faces 1/4 behind the post faces, bottoms flush with the case underside at 5 in.
- **Top** 65-1/2 x 24-3/4 x 3/4, 1/4 in off the back wall and off each side wall; past the posts 1-1/2 at the ends, 3/4 at the front and 1-3/4 at the back. Overall 65-1/2 x 24-3/4 x 17 with the cushion. (Version 1 was 64 wide with 1 in of air at the ends and flush with the posts at the back.)
- **Drawers**: boxes 27-11/16 x 6-1/2 x 18 (5-17/32 inside), the 5 in of clear floor coming out of the height at the fixed 17 in seat. Inside depth from the fronts' back faces to the back's inner face is 516.1 mm against Blum's 481 for the 18 in class on the partition side; on the post side the 471 mm runner body (measured on the drawer bench's 563H4570B) ends 44.6 mm in front of the back, inside the rear bracket's 6 to 48 mm.

Why the rear posts are 2 in off the wall and the runners 18 in (2026-11-01): Brian wanted the baseboard notch gone and the top 1/4 in from the walls all round. Posts clear of the baseboard cost the case 1-3/4 in of depth, which the 21 in runners could not spare (557 mm minimum against 560.6), so Brian picked 18 in runners over pulling the case 1-1/2 in forward or the baseboard out. The 18 in runner then set the post position from the other side: the Blum rear bracket wants the runner's tail within 48 mm of the back, and posts at 1-3/4 in off the wall left 51 mm, so they stand at 2 in (44.6 mm, and 3/4 in to spare over the unmeasured baseboard). The 1/2 in back stays.

## Clear floor, 5 in

Roomba heights: j7 and Combo j7+ 3.4 in, i7 and the 600/900 series 3.6 in, the 2025 lidar models (Plus 405/505) about 3.3 to 3.9 in, the Max 705 fits a 4 in gap, other lidar robots 3.8 to 4.0 in. Five inches leaves an inch over the tallest and room for a rug under the bench. Sources: https://www.irobot.com/en_US/roomba-j7-robot-vacuum/J715020.html, https://irobotthailand.com/en/shop/irobot-roomba-i7/, https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/vacuum-cleaners/irobot-roomba-max-705/m417680/, https://www.sylvane.com/collections/robotic-vacuum-cleaners/products/irobot-roomba-plus-415-combo-vacuum-mop-autowash-dock.

## Model and pipeline

`freestanding_bench.py` (build123d, mm inside, inch constants, same axes and conventions as `built_in_bench.py`): PARTS/INST/HW, asserts for one solid per part, no overlaps, the post volumes against the box minus chamfers minus grooves, the chamfer clear of the grooves, every housed tongue by probe, the groove stop below the tongue and above the panel bottom, the panels' un-housed bottoms on the post faces, the bottom's notches clear of the posts, the baseboard envelope inside the notch, the clear floor (nothing but the posts below 5 in), the Blum rules (box height, inside depth, bracket range at 533 and 547, runner screws inside the post, hook bore and notch probes). `EXPORT=1` writes `cutlist.md`/`.csv` (15 part rows, hardware with the rear brackets, banding, cushion) and `freestanding_bench.step`.

`hero_shot.py` (viewer), `assembly_shots.py` (exploded view, 10 steps: posts, end assemblies, back and cleat, bottom, partition, slides and brackets, boxes, fronts and pulls, top, cushion; end, drawer and front explosions), `part_drawings.py` (sheets in `images/parts/`, every header checked against the cut list), `make_cutlist_page.py` (shop page), `make_pdf.py`, `photo_match.py` (the built-in's camera match on this model; `check` measures the brass pulls and the front posts' maple columns), `render_in_room.py` (jobs guided, styled, dogs, soffit-paint), `leg_options.py` (the five post options for the companion).
