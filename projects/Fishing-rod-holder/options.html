Rafter Rod Holder

A printed replacement for the two notched 1x4 combs on your garage joists. Your rods stay put until you pull a neighbour, then any bump drops one. Everything below is drawn to scale from one parameter file; the grams and times are from real slices (PETG, 2 walls, 15% infill, 0.30 mm layers).

Decided 2026-11-07

Deep hook, as individual printed hooks screwed to a 1x4, with a slot that leans away from the wood and no top bar. Brian's words: "use the deep hook design ... make individual deep hooks, that can be screwed into a 1x4 piece of wood", then "make the bottom resting part of the hook have a slot that is angled to help keep the rod there better", "remove the top most part of the device that is not over the wood", "the slot should angle away from the wood", "5 holders should fit in the 20 in. There should be no space between each hook as it sits on the wood", and "print these vertically so there can be much more in a single print." PETG. The hook is built and checked below; the concept round is kept underneath for the record.

Built · the rod hook

One printed part, 4 x 4 x 0.375 in (4 x 4 x 3/8), an open J. Five of them butted end to end fill the board's 20 in hang with no gap, so the tab is 4 in tall and the rods land every 4 in. The tab screws to the face of a 1x4 with two #8 x 1 in screws, 1 in from the board edge. The jaw reaches 2.25 in past the edge and carries the slot, which leans 35 degrees away from the wood as it goes down: the rod drops in at the top, slides down the ramp away from the board and comes to rest under the lip's edge. To leave it has to come back up the ramp toward the board, then over the lip tip. The lip top slopes up to the tip, so a rod set on the lip rolls in. The top bar over the mouth is gone. The rod lies beside the wood and never touches it; the board hangs from the joist exactly as your combs do.

Checks baked into the script

  • Hold. Pose-space check: the rod centre must rise 1.57 in (1/4 blank), 1.57 in (3/8) and 1.57 in (5/8) before any path to the outside opens; the plain deep hook gave 1.00. A bump moves a rod millimetres, and a hop lands it back on the ramp, which slides it home.
  • Way in. Every blank size reaches the slot from outside with no limit on height, so nothing is trapped either way.
  • Clearances. Seated rods of all three sizes touch neither the hook's walls beyond their resting contact nor the board; the hook sits flat on the board face.
  • Printable standing. The hook prints on its 4 in bottom edge, face vertical, so ten fit one plate. Steepest unsupported surface: 35 degrees from vertical (the slot lean), under the 45 degree limit; bottom corners made square so the whole edge sits on the bed; the screw holes print sideways (5 mm bridges, fine). Thinnest wall 1/8 in (3.2 mm) against the 1.24 mm minimum, lip 0.58 in at its thinnest. Layers run across the hook, so a sideways yank on the lip loads the layer bond; at rod loads that is a 3x margin.
  • Exported STL is watertight, one solid, 63.9 cm3.

How we got here · the concept round, 2026-11-07

Why they fall out

A half-round cut into a vertical edge has its lowest point at the edge. The rod rolls down the arc and rests at the mouth with nothing in front of it. A hook pocket puts the floor inside the plate behind a lip, so a rod has to rise over the lip before it can leave.

The comb in place

Same idea as your boards: a plate screwed to the side face of the joist, hanging straight down, rods lying across two of them. It is 2-1/2 in wide and 1/2 in thick, hangs 17 in below the joist (yours hang 20), and holds four rods 4 in apart. The mouths face the same way your notches do.

A · Hook pocket

5/8 inlift to leave
  • Level mouth: slide the rod in, let it drop.
  • Any blank from 1/4 to 5/8 in sits in the 7/8 in pocket.
  • No moving parts, nothing to relax over the years.
Picked

B · Deep hook

1 inlift to leave
  • Mouth higher than the lip: the rod rolls into the pocket by itself.
  • Harder to lift out overhead; each pocket is 3/8 in taller, so the comb grows or the pitch does.
  • Then reworked on Brian's ask into the open J with the angled slot (built section above).

What I assumed, and what the side view asks

  • Joists 2x8 at 16 in on centre (the 7 in of board above the joist bottom matched a 2x8).
  • Rods per comb 4, like your longer board. Modules add two at a time.
  • Pitch 4 in, first rod 4 in below the joist. If reels stay on and line up, go 5 in or stagger the handles.
  • Blank at the comb 1/4 to 5/8 in; pocket 7/8 in, mouth 1-1/4 in tall.
  • Hang 17 in clears the open door the way your 20 in does.
  • Mouth faces the same way as your notches; say so if it should flip.
  • Screws three #8 x 1-1/2 into the joist, solid pads around every hole in the 3MF.
  • Comb spacing unknown; it only matters for the rods, not the print.
